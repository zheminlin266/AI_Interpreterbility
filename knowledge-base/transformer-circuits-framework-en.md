## What problem does the paper tackle?

How can we turn Transformer weights and activations into paths we can inspect, then use those paths to explain a specific prediction behavior? The authors study simplified zero-, one-, and two-layer models. The main examples omit MLPs to isolate interactions between attention heads. The paper contributes both algebraic decompositions and empirical analyses of particular models. Expanding the equations does not guarantee a compact human explanation for every term. [Model simplifications](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#model-simplifications)

## Three tools: residual streams, path expansion, and QK versus OV

- **The residual stream is a communication channel.** Attention heads add their outputs to the residual stream, which downstream components can read. Multiplying an upstream output matrix by a downstream input matrix gives an effective connection, which the paper calls a virtual weight. It identifies a possible communication path; whether that path matters on a given input still depends on the activations and intervention evidence. [Residual stream and virtual weights](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#residual-comms)
- **Path expansion separates the logit by computational route.** A zero-layer model maps the current token’s embedding directly to output scores and can approximate bigram statistics. In a one-layer attention-only model, logits decompose into a direct term `W_U W_E` and individual attention-head contributions. Deeper models add paths across heads. When decomposing OV paths on a forward pass, we can hold that run’s attention patterns fixed; this does not mean the patterns are constant across inputs. [Zero-layer model](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#zero-layer-transformers) · [One-layer path expansion](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#onel-path-expansion)
- **QK tells a head where to read; OV tells it what to write.** QK scores queries against keys, with candidate positions competing through the attention softmax. OV transforms the content read from those positions. Folding the embedding and output maps into the one-layer analysis exposes skip-trigrams such as `A … B → C`: current token `B` attends to an earlier `A`, which changes the next-token logit for `C`. These are context-dependent score contributions, not independent prediction probabilities. [QK and OV circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#splitting-attention-head-terms-into-circuits) · [Skip-trigrams](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#interpretation-as-skip-trigrams)

## How do two layers implement an induction head?

A later head can use an earlier head’s write to construct its **Q, K, or V**. Q/K composition changes attention matching; V composition changes what is moved once a source position is selected. The classic induction circuit uses K-composition. In `A B … A`, a previous-token head first writes information about the earlier `A` at the `B` position. The later induction head constructs a key containing information about `A` at that position. The final `A` can then match the key at `B`, and the induction head’s OV path boosts `B` as the next token. Compared with copying based only on the current token and an earlier token in a one-layer model, the circuit adds a step: **find a previous match, then take the token that followed it**. [Three kinds of composition](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#three-kinds-of-composition) · [How induction heads work](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#how-induction-heads-work)

## What does the evidence support?

- **Weight analysis supports the proposed mechanism.** The authors use an eigenvalue statistic as a working indicator for QK matching and OV copying in candidate induction heads. An attention plot or a single matrix statistic does not establish the entire algorithm. [Checking the mechanistic theory](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#checking-the-mechanistic-theory)
- **The ablation has specific conditions.** The authors freeze the observed attention patterns and compare how different path orders affect loss. Second-order OV paths had a small marginal effect in the two-layer model analyzed. This result concerns V-composition; it does not rule out Q/K composition or generalize to deeper models. [Term importance analysis](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#term-importance-analysis)
- **Read the original plot alongside the erratum.** The original composition plot missed some interactions because an underlying library incorrectly reordered matrix factors when computing a Frobenius norm. In the corrected plot, K-composition with the previous-token head remains important, and two induction heads also compose with a head that reads several preceding tokens. The minimal two-head circuit is not the only implementation. [Attention head composition erratum](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#comment-errata)
- **Decomposition is not full understanding.** Even a one-layer model expanded into vocabulary-space matrices leaves many entries to summarize. These experiments do not establish how to explain MLPs and more complex interactions in large models or how far the results generalize across tasks. [What it means to understand a one-layer model](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#do-we-fully-understand-one-layer-models) · [Scope and implications](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#where-does-this-leave-us)

## Team and contributions

The contribution statement credits Nelson Elhage, Catherine Olsson, Neel Nanda, and Chris Olah with developing the framework together. The examples it gives include Elhage finding evidence that informed the residual-stream communication framing, Olsson characterizing neurons and attention heads, Nanda advancing the understanding of attention-head composition, and Olah developing foundational ideas and discovering induction heads. These are project-specific contributions recorded at publication, not exclusive roles or current affiliations. [Author contributions](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html#author-contributions)
